Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-11-04

This policy explains what the Road Rage game for iPhone, iPad and Android (“the game”) collects and how it is used. Questions: support@roadrage99.com.

The short version: we run no servers of our own that store player data. What this page describes goes to Unity, Tenjin, Apple or Google, as each section says. The game has no accounts of its own, and we do not ask for your name, email, contact list, location, camera or microphone. Your progress is saved on your device and, if you are signed in to Game Center and iCloud (iPhone and iPad) or Google Play Games (Android), in a cloud save that Apple or Google keeps for you, as described in sections 1 and 2. The game shows ads, and the ad service may collect some data, as described in section 3. To measure our own ads the game may use Tenjin, as described in “Ad measurement” in section 3. The game downloads its settings from Unity, as described in section 5. Purchases go through Apple or Google, and Unity IAP sends Unity a record of each one, as described in section 6. The game does not send crash reports (section 7). In some places the game asks you first: see “Your privacy choices” below. Play stats are off until you say yes.

Your privacy choices

If your device's region is set to Canada, a country of the European Union or the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, or if the game cannot tell what it is set to, the game shows a privacy card over the menu the first time you open it. The game goes by your device's region setting.

The card has three separate choices: PLAY STATS, PERSONALIZED ADS and AD MEASUREMENT. Each one is off until you turn it on. NO THANKS saves all three as off, even one you had switched on; DRIVE saves them as you set them. The game plays the same whatever you choose, and nothing is offered in exchange for a yes. The card is in English or French: it follows your device's language, and the FRANÇAIS / ENGLISH button on the card switches it.

You can change any of the three at any time in OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY (CONFIDENTIALITÉ in French). Your answers are stored only on your device. If you delete and reinstall the game, it may ask again.

Until you answer the card, the game does not start any of these: the settings download from Unity (section 5) and Unity's services, play stats (section 4), the iOS tracking question, the ad service (section 3) and Tenjin (“Ad measurement”). It still connects to the App Store or Google Play (section 6), and signs you in to Game Center or Google Play Games if you use them (section 2).

Play stats

  • What it is. Which levels you play, how far you get and how runs end, so we can improve the game. Section 4 has the full list.
  • Who gets it. Unity, through Unity Analytics, as our service provider.
  • If you turn it on. Unity receives those events with a random ID for your install, a random ID for each play session, technical details about your device and, on iPhone and iPad, Apple's identifier for vendor (IDFV). Unity also receives your IP address when your device contacts its servers.
  • If you leave it off. No play stats are sent.
  • To change it. OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY. Turning it off stops collection and asks Unity to delete what was already sent for your install. If the game cannot reach Unity at that moment, it asks again the next time it starts.

Personalized ads

  • What it is. Whether our ad partners may use your advertising ID and how you use the game to choose the ads you see.
  • Who gets it. Unity LevelPlay and the ironSource and Unity Ads networks it connects. Section 3 lists what they may collect.
  • If you turn it on. On iPhone and iPad, iOS then asks whether the game may track you. iOS's answer has the last word: if you tap Ask App Not to Track, the game treats this choice as off, and the card shows “Turned off in iOS Settings.”
  • If you leave it off. You still see ads. The game tells the ad service that you have not agreed to personalized ads, and in Canada also that your data must not be sold or shared, so the ads you see should not be personalized. When an ad is requested or shown, the ad service still receives information such as your IP address and device details (section 3).
  • If you allowed tracking in iOS before this card existed. Leaving this off tells the ad service not to personalize, but iOS still reports your earlier answer to apps. To change that, go to iOS Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Tracking.
  • To change it. OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY. A change applies to the ads requested after it, and may only take full effect the next time you start the game.

Ad measurement

  • What it is. Tenjin, a measurement service, lets us learn whether one of our own ads led to your install. Section 3 has the details.
  • If you turn it on. On iPhone and iPad, iOS then asks whether the game may track you. If you tap Ask App Not to Track, Tenjin stays off, and the card shows “Turned off in iOS Settings.” If you later allow tracking in iOS Settings, it can start.
  • If you leave it off. The game does not start Tenjin.
  • To change it. OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY. Turning it off tells Tenjin to stop, and the game no longer starts Tenjin after that.

The iOS tracking question

Where the game shows the card, iOS asks whether the game may track you only after you turn on PERSONALIZED ADS or AD MEASUREMENT, and never before you answer the card. If you say no to everything, or turn on PLAY STATS only, iOS does not ask.

What still runs if you say no to everything

The game keeps working, and these still run. We treat them as needed to run the game, or as part of Apple's or Google's own systems:

  • Game settings from Unity. After you answer, whatever you answer, the game downloads its settings from Unity Remote Config, as described in section 5. This sends a random ID for your install and device details.
  • Purchases. The game connects to the App Store or Google Play so it can sell and restore purchases. If you start or make a purchase, Unity IAP sends Unity a record of it, as described in section 6.
  • Game Center and Google Play Games. If you are signed in, the game uses them as described in section 2. You can play without signing in.
  • Ads. You still see ads, as described in “Personalized ads” above and in section 3.
  • Apple's install reports (iPhone and iPad). These come from SKAdNetwork, Apple's own system: Apple sends anonymous install counts to ad networks, and we receive no data about you from it.

Everywhere else

If your device's region is anywhere else (for example the United States or the Philippines), the game does not show this card. It downloads its settings from Unity each time it starts (section 5). On iPhone and iPad, iOS asks whether the game may track you soon after you first open it (section 3). The first time you open the menu, the game asks “help improve Road Rage?” about play stats, which stay off until you say yes (section 4). Tenjin may run without a question there, limited by your iOS tracking answer or your Android ad settings (“Ad measurement” in section 3). OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY then has the play stats choice only.

1. Your progress and the cloud save

Your progress (stars, best times, your Gas Money, the cars you own and their upgrades, your selected car, your achievements, settings, whether you own Remove Ads, and your privacy choices) is saved locally in the game's storage on your device.

Cloud save. If you are signed in to Game Center and iCloud on iPhone or iPad, the game also keeps a copy of your progress in iCloud (Apple's key-value storage, under your Apple ID). If you are signed in to Google Play Games on Android, it keeps the copy in Google Play Games' saved games, under your Google account. This lets your progress follow you to your other devices and come back after a reinstall. Apple or Google stores this copy for you; we do not receive it. Your privacy choices are not part of the cloud save. If you are not signed in, nothing leaves your device this way, and deleting the game deletes your progress.

2. Game Center and Google Play Games

If you are signed in to Game Center (iPhone and iPad) or Google Play Games (Android), the game uses it for achievements, leaderboards and the cloud save. The game reads your player name and player ID from Apple or Google to show them in the game and to keep different players' progress apart. It sends Apple or Google the achievements you earn and your best times for the leaderboards. Leaderboards show your player name and times to other players, following your Game Center or Play Games settings. On iOS 26 and later the game also starts and ends a Game Center activity for each level you drive, and on Android it reports Play Games events: levels started, levels finished and stars earned. Apple and Google handle this data under their own policies: Apple and Google. You can play without signing in; the game then keeps your progress on your device only. If you are signed in, this starts at the menu, including before you answer the privacy card.

3. Ads

The game is free and shows two kinds of ads: rewarded ads you choose to watch to start a run with a power, and interstitial ads that can appear between runs. Ads are served through Unity LevelPlay (a mediation service from Unity, which includes the ironSource and Unity Ads networks) and the ad networks it connects. The ad service starts when you start your first level. When an ad is requested or shown, these companies may collect information such as your device's IP address, device and operating system details, the ad you saw and how you interacted with it, and, if you allow it, your device's advertising identifier (the IDFA on iPhone and iPad, the Android advertising ID on Android). They use it to choose and show ads, measure them, prevent fraud and, depending on your choice, personalize ads.

Tracking question (iOS). iOS can ask whether the game may track you across other companies' apps and websites, using Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. Where the game shows the privacy card, iOS asks only if you turned on PERSONALIZED ADS or AD MEASUREMENT, and never before you answer the card. Elsewhere, iOS asks soon after you first open the game. If you tap Ask App Not to Track, the game does not get your advertising identifier and ads still appear, but they are less relevant. You can change this any time in iOS Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Tracking.

Advertising ID (Android). On Android the ad services may use your Android advertising ID, a resettable ID you can see and control in Android Settings, then Privacy (or Google, then Ads). If you delete your advertising ID or opt out of ads personalization there, the game gets a blank ID and ads still appear, but they are less relevant.

Personalized ads and consent. Where the game shows the privacy card, ads may be personalized only if you turn on PERSONALIZED ADS (see “Your privacy choices”), and iOS's tracking answer allows it. In the European Union, the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, your consent is our legal basis for personalized ads. If you decline, you still see ads, just not personalized ones, and in Canada the game also tells the ad service that your data must not be sold or shared. Your answer is stored only on your device, and you can change it any time in OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY. Everywhere, the game tells the ad services that it is not directed at children.

The ad companies act on their own behalf for some of this data. Their policies: Unity, including ironSource and Unity LevelPlay.

You can remove between-run ads with the Remove Ads purchase (section 6).

Ad measurement (Tenjin)

We may advertise Road Rage in other apps. To learn whether one of those ads led to an install, the game can use Tenjin, a measurement service. We switch Tenjin on and off through the game's settings from Unity (section 5), so it may not be running at all.

When it runs. Where the game shows the privacy card, only if you turned on AD MEASUREMENT and, on iPhone and iPad, iOS's tracking answer allows it. Elsewhere the game does not ask: Tenjin can run, limited by your iOS tracking answer or your Android ad settings. Without your permission there it gets no advertising identifier, but it still receives the other details below.

What it receives. When you open the game: your IP address; the platform and operating system version; your device type and model; your device's language and country settings (locale and country); the app's bundle ID and version; the Tenjin SDK version; your Limit Ad Tracking setting; on iPhone and iPad, Apple's identifier for vendor (IDFV) and, if it is turned on, Apple Search Ads attribution (whether an Apple Search Ads ad led to the install); and your advertising identifier if you allowed it (iOS: you allowed tracking; Android: you have not opted out of ads personalization). On Android it also receives the Google Play install referrer (which ad or link led to the install) and the App Set ID. It receives a new session when you come back to the game after a minute or more away, and the revenue of the ads shown in the game, so we can see whether our own ads pay for themselves. We do not send your purchases to Tenjin.

Apple's install reports. On iPhone and iPad, SKAdNetwork is Apple's own system: Apple sends anonymous install counts to ad networks, and we receive no data about you from it.

Tenjin stores this data to process it for us, as our service provider (a data processor), not for its own purposes. To see, correct or delete it, write to us (section 10), not to Tenjin. Its privacy policy: tenjin.com/privacy.

4. Play stats (optional, off until you say yes)

Where the game shows the privacy card, this is its PLAY STATS choice. Elsewhere, the first time you open the menu, the game asks “help improve Road Rage?”. Nothing is sent until you say yes. If you do, the game uses Unity Analytics to record events so we can tune difficulty and see where players get stuck:

  • when you start, finish, fail or quit a level, with the level name and number, the game mode, the car you picked, whether Free Drive was on, your steering setting, how many seconds you played, how far you got, which try it was, the power you held (if any), whether you started with a Super Juggernaut or a free power on the house, whether you finished or skipped the Practice Lot, your stars (on a win), why you failed (on a loss), how long you were stuck in traffic, how full your ROAD RAGE meter was at the finish, your heart attacks, the Capn Snafu virtues you held, and whether you quit to the menu or to retry;
  • how you play: each RAGE MODE (cars smashed, how long the meter sat full, how close you came to a heart attack), the Practice Lot's steps and first-time hints, the back alleys you drive through, the story scenes you watch or skip, the achievements you earn, your picks in Capn Snafu's garden, milestones on your lifetime smash tally, and the games you play on the garage TV (which game, your score, how long and how it ended);
  • when you watch an ad (rewarded or between runs) and whether you earned the reward; for ads between runs and ads that load, the ad network and ad unit that served it, how long it showed and whether you tapped it;
  • when you open a store screen, tap a buy button, or a purchase fails (with the reason) or completes, with the product, the screen you bought it from, your Gas Money before the purchase, how many levels you had finished and how many purchases you made that session.

These events are not tied to your name, Apple ID or Google account. Unity Analytics adds a random ID for your install and a random ID for each play session, records when a session starts and ends (and a short signal every minute while you play), and receives some technical details automatically: your device model, operating system version, app version, language and region setting, time zone, screen size, processor, graphics chip and memory size, battery level, sound volume, and, on iPhone and iPad, Apple's identifier for vendor (IDFV). Unity also receives your IP address when your device contacts its servers, and uses it to work out your approximate country. We use Unity as a service provider to process this data for us. Unity keeps it for 13 months by default, then deletes it. Unity's own handling is described in its Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. None of this is sent unless you said yes to play stats.

You can turn this off any time: OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY. Turning it off stops collection and sends Unity a request to delete the data already sent for your install (if the game cannot reach Unity then, it asks again the next time it starts); Unity may keep IP addresses in its security logs for a limited time.

5. Game settings from Unity

The game downloads its current settings (for example, how often an ad may appear between runs, and whether Tenjin is switched on) from Unity Remote Config, part of Unity Gaming Services. Where the game shows the privacy card, the first download waits until you answer the card. After that, and everywhere else, it happens each time the game starts, whatever you answered and whether or not you said yes to play stats, because the game needs these settings to work as intended. Starting Unity's services also lets Unity IAP download its own settings from Unity.

The request sends: a random ID that Unity creates for your install (it stays the same until you delete the game), our Unity project ID, the game's app ID and version, the platform, your device's model, operating system version and language, its processor (type, number of cores and speed), memory and graphics memory size, graphics chip (name, maker and version), screen resolution and density, graphics capabilities, how the game was installed and from which store, whether the device is jailbroken or rooted, whether the game is a test build, and the version of Unity's Remote Config software. Unity receives your IP address when your device contacts its servers. It does not include your advertising identifier or your play stats. We use this only to send the game its settings, not to identify you or for advertising. Unity processes it for us as a service provider, under the Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

6. Purchases

The game is free, and you can make optional purchases in it: Remove Ads, Gas Money packs (the game's currency), cars, and Super Juggernaut starts (packs of three). None of them is a subscription. On iPhone and iPad they go through Apple's In-App Purchase, and on Android through Google Play Billing, both using Unity IAP. Apple or Google handles your payment; we never see your card or billing details. The game checks with the App Store or Google Play whether you own a purchase (including for Restore Purchases) and remembers what you own, along with your Gas Money and upgrades, in the save described in section 1. If you said yes to play stats, we also record the purchase steps listed in section 4, without payment details.

Unity IAP's purchase records. Whether or not you said yes to play stats, Unity IAP sends Unity a record of each step of a purchase: when one starts, fails or completes (and, rarely, at launch if an earlier order was still pending). The record includes the product, its price and currency, the transaction ID, the store's signed purchase record (receipt), a random ID for your install and session, when the game was installed, your device model and language, the app, engine and SDK versions and, on iPhone and iPad, Apple's identifier for vendor (IDFV). It does not include your advertising identifier or your card or billing details. Unity processes it under the Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

7. Crash reports and diagnostics

The game does not send crash reports or diagnostics to us or to Unity: Unity's crash and diagnostics reporting and its hardware statistics are turned off in the game. If you have chosen in your device's settings to share analytics or crash data with app developers (on iPhone and iPad, Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements; on Android, your Google usage and diagnostics setting), Apple or Google may give us crash reports and performance figures for the game. They collect and share that data under their own policies, and it does not include your name or contact details.

8. What we do not collect

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location, microphone or camera data. The game has no accounts of its own and no chat; the only social features are the Game Center and Google Play Games achievements and leaderboards in section 2. We do not sell your data. This website has no analytics or tracking scripts; see section 11.

9. Children

Road Rage is not directed at children. It is intended for players 13 and older (App Store and Google Play age rating 13+) and is not in Apple's Kids category or Google Play's Designed for Families program. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you are a parent and believe your child under 13 has given us information, email us and we will delete it.

10. Your choices and rights

  • Change play stats, personalized ads and ad measurement in OPTIONS > GAME > PRIVACY. Where the game doesn't show the privacy card, this has play stats only.
  • Say no to tracking in the iOS prompt, or change it in iOS Settings. On Android, opt out of ads personalization in Android Settings.
  • Turning off play stats also requests deletion of the play stats already sent.
  • Play without signing in to Game Center or Google Play Games, or sign out in your device's settings, so the game sends no achievements or best times and keeps no cloud save. You can manage the cloud copy of your progress in your iCloud or Google Play Games settings.
  • Reset your advertising identifier in iOS Settings or Android Settings.
  • Buy Remove Ads to stop between-run ads.
  • Your requests. To see, correct or delete data a service holds for this game, email support@roadrage99.com. We pass your request to Tenjin and Unity, which hold that data for us. Tell us your device and platform (for example iPhone, iPad or Android) and roughly when you played, because the data is tied to random IDs rather than your name. Depending on where you live (for example Canada, including Quebec, the EU, the UK or California) you may have additional rights to access, correct, delete or object, or to withdraw your consent.

11. This website

This site is plain pages: no cookies, no analytics, no tracking scripts, no external fonts or CDNs. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub may log visitor IP addresses as any web host does (GitHub's privacy statement). If you email us, we use your address only to reply.

12. Changes

If we change this policy we will change the date above and, for material changes, tell you in the game or on this site. This policy is also available en français.
