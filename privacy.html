Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-11-15

This policy explains what the Road Rage game for iPhone, iPad and Android (“the game”) collects and how it is used. It describes version 1.1.10 of the game and later, on the App Store and in testing (TestFlight and Google Play testing). Some players still have an older version, because an update reaches the App Store some time after our testers get it; section 12 lists how older versions differ. Questions: support@roadrage99.com.

The short version: the game has no accounts of its own, and we do not ask for your name, email, contact list, location, camera or microphone. The first time you open the game it shows a privacy card with three switches, all off until you turn them on: anonymous play stats, personalized ads and ad measurement. Nothing that sends data to Unity, the ad services or the ad measurement service starts until you answer, and the game plays the same whatever you choose. Your progress is saved on your device and, if you are signed in to Game Center and iCloud (iPhone and iPad) or Google Play Games (Android), in a cloud save that Apple or Google keeps for you, as described in sections 1 and 2. The game shows ads, and the ad service may collect some data, as described in section 3. After you answer the card, the game downloads its settings from Unity, as described in section 5. The game does not send crash reports (section 7).

Your privacy choices

The first time you open the game, before anything that sends data starts, it shows a card called YOUR CHOICES, in English or French (it follows your phone's language, and the FRANÇAIS / ENGLISH button on the card switches it). It has three switches, all off until you turn them on:

  • PLAY STATS: anonymous events about how you play, sent to Unity Analytics (section 4).
  • PERSONALIZED ADS: whether the ad services may use your advertising identifier and how you use the game to choose the ads you see (section 3).
  • AD MEASUREMENT: whether Tenjin may tell us that one of our own ads led to your install (section 3).

DRIVE saves your switches as they are, and NO THANKS turns all three off. Until you answer, the game does not start Unity's services (play stats and the settings download in section 5), the ad service, ad measurement, or the iOS tracking question. After any answer, NO THANKS included, the game plays in full and shows ads that are not personalized. Your answers are stored only on your device, and you can change them any time in Options, GAME, PRIVACY. A few things do not wait for the card, because they send nothing to us or to the ad services: Game Center or Google Play Games sign-in (section 2), the connection to the App Store or Google Play for purchases (section 6), and, on iPhone and iPad, Apple's install registration (section 3).

1. Your progress and the cloud save

Your progress (stars, best times, your Gas Money, the cars you own and their upgrades, your selected car, your achievements, settings, whether you own Remove Ads, and your privacy choices) is saved locally in the game's storage on your device.

Cloud save. If you are signed in to Game Center and iCloud on iPhone or iPad, the game also keeps a copy of your progress in iCloud (Apple's key-value storage, under your Apple ID). If you are signed in to Google Play Games on Android, it keeps the copy in Google Play Games' saved games, under your Google account. This lets your progress follow you to your other devices and come back after a reinstall. Apple or Google stores this copy for you; we do not receive it. If you are not signed in, nothing leaves your device this way, and deleting the game deletes your progress.

2. Game Center and Google Play Games

If you are signed in to Game Center (iPhone and iPad) or Google Play Games (Android), the game uses it for achievements, leaderboards and the cloud save. The game reads your player name and player ID from Apple or Google to show them in the game and to keep different players' progress apart. It sends Apple or Google the achievements you earn and your best times and scores for the leaderboards: your level times, your Stunt Park lap and big-air records, and your scores in the garage TV games. Leaderboards show your player name and times or scores to other players, following your Game Center or Play Games settings. On iOS 26 and later the game also starts and ends a Game Center activity for each level you drive, and on Android it reports Play Games events: levels started, levels finished and stars earned. Apple and Google handle this data under their own policies: Apple and Google. You can play without signing in; the game then keeps your progress on your device only.

3. Ads and ad measurement

The game is free and shows two kinds of ads: rewarded ads you choose to watch to start a run with a power, and interstitial ads that can appear between runs. Ads are served through Unity LevelPlay (a mediation service from Unity, which includes the ironSource and Unity Ads networks) and the ad networks it connects. The ad service starts only after you answer the privacy card. When an ad is requested or shown, these companies may collect information such as your device's IP address, device and operating system details, the ad you saw and how you interacted with it, and, if you allow it, your device's advertising identifier (the IDFA on iPhone and iPad, the Android advertising ID on Android). They use it to choose and show ads, measure them, prevent fraud and, depending on your choice, personalize ads.

Personalized ads. The PERSONALIZED ADS switch on the privacy card decides whether the game tells the ad services they may personalize ads. Your consent is our legal basis for personalized ads. If it is off, you still see ads, just not personalized ones, and the game also sends the ad services the “do not sell or share” signal used under U.S. state privacy laws. On iPhone and iPad, iOS's own tracking answer (below) comes first: if you tell iOS not to track, ads are not personalized even with the switch on, and the card says the switch is turned off in iOS Settings. The game always tells the ad services that it is not directed at children.

Tracking question (iOS). iOS asks whether the game may track you across other companies' apps and websites, using Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. The game shows it only after you turn on PERSONALIZED ADS or AD MEASUREMENT; if you leave both off, iOS never asks. If you tap Ask App Not to Track, the game does not get your advertising identifier and ads still appear, but they are less relevant. You can change this any time in iOS Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Tracking.

Advertising ID (Android). On Android the ad services may use your Android advertising ID, a resettable ID you can see and control in Android Settings, then Privacy (or Google, then Ads). If you delete your advertising ID or opt out of ads personalization there, the game gets a blank ID and ads still appear, but they are less relevant.

Ad measurement (Tenjin). We advertise Road Rage in other apps. If you turn on AD MEASUREMENT, the game starts the measurement service Tenjin, which tells us whether one of our own ads led to your install. The game reports to Tenjin that the game was installed and opened, a new session when you come back to the game after a minute or more away, and details of each ad you see in the game, such as the ad network, ad unit, country and the revenue the ad earned. It does not report purchases. Tenjin's software also collects, on its own, your device and operating system details, IP address, app version and language, and your advertising identifier if you allowed tracking (iOS) or have not deleted or limited it (Android); on Android it also reads the Google Play install referrer and your app set ID. On iPhone and iPad, Tenjin starts only if you also allow tracking in the iOS prompt. We switch measurement on and off from our side, so even with AD MEASUREMENT on, nothing may be sent. If you turn AD MEASUREMENT off later, the game tells Tenjin to stop and sends nothing more. Tenjin processes this data to provide the measurement for us; see Tenjin's privacy policy.

Apple's install registration (iPhone and iPad). When you first open the game, it registers the install once with Apple's SKAdNetwork, whatever you choose on the privacy card. If you installed the game from an ad, this lets Apple tell the ad network that showed it, and Tenjin, that the ad led to an install. Apple sends these reports itself, under its own policy. They contain no advertising identifier or other device identifier, and the game adds no data of its own.

The ad companies act on their own behalf for some of this data. Their policies: ironSource / Unity LevelPlay and Unity.

You can remove between-run ads with the Remove Ads purchase (section 6).

4. Anonymous play stats (optional, off until you say yes)

Play stats are the PLAY STATS switch on the privacy card. Nothing is sent unless you turn it on. If you do, the game uses Unity Analytics to record anonymous events so we can tune difficulty and see where players get stuck:

  • when you start, finish, fail or quit a level, with the level name and number, the car you picked, whether Free Drive was on, your steering setting, how many seconds you played, how far you got, which try it was, the power you held (if any), whether you started with a Super Juggernaut, your stars (on a win), why you failed (on a loss), how long you were stuck in traffic, how full your ROAD RAGE meter was at the finish, your heart attacks, and whether you quit to the menu or to retry;
  • how you play: each RAGE MODE (cars smashed, how long the meter sat full), the Practice Lot's steps and first-time hints, the back alleys you drive through, the story scenes you watch or skip, the achievements you earn, your picks in Capn Snafu's garden, milestones on your lifetime smash tally, each round of a garage TV game (which game, your score, how long you played, and how the round ended or which forecast you picked on the weather channel), and, in test versions, each RAGE RODEO arena match (the difficulty, clock, car, result, grade, wrecks and length);
  • when you watch an ad (rewarded or between runs) and whether you earned the reward; for ads between runs and ads that load, the ad network and ad unit that served it, how long it showed and whether you tapped it;
  • when you open a store screen, tap a buy button, or a purchase fails (with the reason) or completes, with the product, the screen you bought it from, your Gas Money before the purchase, how many levels you had finished and how many purchases you made that session.

These events are not tied to your name, Apple ID or Google account. Unity Analytics adds a random ID for your install and a random ID for each play session, and it also receives some technical details automatically: your device model, operating system version, app version, language, time zone, screen size, processor, graphics chip and memory size, battery level, and, on iPhone and iPad, Apple's per-developer device identifier (the identifier for vendor, IDFV). Unity also receives your IP address when your device contacts its servers, and uses it to work out your approximate country. We use Unity as a service provider to process this data for us. Unity keeps it for 13 months by default, then deletes it. Unity's own handling is described in its Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. None of this is sent unless you turned on PLAY STATS.

You can turn this off any time: Options, GAME, PRIVACY, then switch off PLAY STATS and tap SAVE. Turning it off stops collection and sends Unity a request to delete the data already sent for your install; Unity may keep IP addresses in its security logs for a limited time.

5. Game settings from Unity

After you answer the privacy card, whatever you choose, and then each time it starts, the game downloads its current settings (for example, how often an ad may appear between runs, and whether ad measurement is switched on) from Unity Remote Config, part of Unity Gaming Services. This does not depend on PLAY STATS, because the game needs these settings to work as intended. The request includes a random ID for your install, the app version and platform, and technical details about your device such as its model, operating system version, language and screen, and Unity receives your IP address when your device contacts its servers. We use this only to send the game its settings, not to identify you or for advertising. Unity processes it for us as a service provider, under the Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

6. Purchases

The game is free, and you can make optional purchases in it: Remove Ads, Gas Money packs (the game's currency), cars, and Super Juggernaut starts (packs of three). None of them is a subscription. On iPhone and iPad they go through Apple's In-App Purchase, and on Android through Google Play Billing, both using Unity IAP. Apple or Google handles your payment; we never see your card or billing details. The game checks with the App Store or Google Play whether you own a purchase (including for Restore Purchases) and remembers what you own, along with your Gas Money and upgrades, in the save described in section 1. If you turned on play stats, we also record that a purchase happened, without payment details.

7. Crash reports and diagnostics

The game does not send crash reports or diagnostics. Unity's crash and diagnostics reporting (Unity Diagnostics) and its hardware statistics are switched off in every version from 1.1.6 on, so a crash or an error stays on your device. If we add crash reporting later, it will wait for your answer on the privacy card, and we will update this policy first. Older versions did send crash reports; see section 12.

8. What we do not collect

We do not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location, microphone or camera data. The game has no accounts of its own and no chat; the only social features are the Game Center and Google Play Games achievements and leaderboards in section 2. We do not sell your data. This website has no analytics or tracking scripts; see section 11.

9. Children

Road Rage is not directed at children. It is intended for players 13 and older (App Store and Google Play age rating 13+) and is not in Apple's Kids category or Google Play's Designed for Families program. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you are a parent and believe your child under 13 has given us information, email us and we will delete it.

10. Your choices and rights

  • Choose PLAY STATS, PERSONALIZED ADS and AD MEASUREMENT on the privacy card, and change them any time in Options, GAME, PRIVACY. Turning off PLAY STATS also requests deletion of your play stats; turning off AD MEASUREMENT tells Tenjin to stop.
  • Say no to tracking in the iOS prompt, or change it in iOS Settings. On Android, opt out of ads personalization in Android Settings.
  • Play without signing in to Game Center or Google Play Games, or sign out in your device's settings, so the game sends no achievements or best times and keeps no cloud save. You can manage the cloud copy of your progress in your iCloud or Google Play Games settings.
  • Reset your advertising identifier in iOS Settings or Android Settings.
  • Buy Remove Ads to stop between-run ads.
  • Email support@roadrage99.com to ask what we hold, or to ask us to delete it. Depending on where you live (for example the EU, UK, Canada or California) you may have additional rights to access, correct, delete or object. Because we cannot identify you from the anonymous stats, we may ask for details such as your install's date and device to find them.

11. This website

This site is plain pages: no cookies, no analytics, no tracking scripts, no external fonts or CDNs. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub may log visitor IP addresses as any web host does (GitHub's privacy statement). If you email us, we use your address only to reply.

12. Changes and older versions

If we change this policy we will change the date above and, for material changes, tell you in the game or on this site.

Older versions. The game shows its version in the bottom-right corner of Options (from 1.1.6), or you can find it on the game's App Store or Google Play page. Older versions differ from the description above in these ways:

  • Before 1.1.5 there is no privacy card. The game asks “help improve Road Rage?” the first time you open the menu, and sends play stats only after you tap yes (turn them off in Options, GAME, SHARE STATS). Only players whose device is set up in the European Union, the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, or whose region the game cannot tell, are asked whether ads may be personalized (change it in Options, GAME, PERSONALIZED ADS); elsewhere iOS's tracking question appears on first launch. The game downloads its settings from Unity Remote Config each time it starts, before any question is answered.
  • From 1.1.5 to 1.1.9 the privacy card is shown only where your device's region is set to Canada, the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, or cannot be told; elsewhere the game asks the play stats question and iOS's tracking question and downloads its settings at launch, as above. In those other regions, ad measurement (Tenjin) does not wait for a yes on the card: if we had switched it on, it could start once iOS's tracking question was answered, without your advertising identifier if you said no to tracking. Options, GAME, PRIVACY replaces SHARE STATS and PERSONALIZED ADS from 1.1.5.
  • Before 1.1.6 the game uses Unity Diagnostics and Unity's hardware statistics. From the moment the game opens, whatever you chose, these send Unity a random installation ID, your device model, operating system and app version, language, memory and graphics details, performance timings, and reports of crashes, errors and freezes (the error message and the code path that failed). Unity also receives your IP address. The reports do not include your name, email or contacts. Unity processes them for us as a service provider, under the Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.
  • Before 1.1.10, because of a setting in one of Unity's software packages, the game could contact Unity's analytics servers when it started, before you answered any question, sending technical details about your device and a random ID for your install. Version 1.1.10 removed this.
